# ProofLab 3–5 person user-testing protocol

## Research question

Can a number-theory student or educator correctly distinguish computational evidence, disproof, proof, and an honestly unresolved result after using ProofLab?

## Participants

Recruit 3–5 real participants from the intended audience:

- undergraduate or graduate mathematics students;
- number-theory educators or teaching assistants;
- researchers who review computational mathematical claims.

Record role and approximate experience, not sensitive personal data.

## Tasks

1. Open the false-family example and predict the result before running it.
2. Explain why one exact counterexample disproves the universal claim.
3. Open the true identity and distinguish symbolic residual `0` from checking many values.
4. Replay the certificate and explain what the checksum does and does not establish.
5. Open the modular impossibility example and explain why exhaustive residues modulo 4 imply global non-existence.
6. Run “Try to break this argument.”
7. Inspect an `UNKNOWN` example and propose a supported reformulation.
8. Export or share an investigation.

## Measures

- task completion without facilitator help;
- time to first correct evidence classification;
- correct classification before and after the session;
- whether the participant can state the GPT/verifier boundary;
- whether certificate replay and `UNKNOWN` are understood;
- one friction point and one valuable moment per participant.
